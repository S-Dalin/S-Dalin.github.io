Why I started writing research notes in public

date2026-08-10
tagsresearch, notes
statusevergreen

Most of what a researcher produces never makes it into a paper. The dead ends, the half-formed ideas, the small tricks that took a week to find — they live in private notebooks and then disappear. This blog is my attempt to keep them alive.

Working with the garage door up

There is a well-known argument for "working with the garage door up": let people see the messy middle of your work, not just the polished result. For research, that means writing down what you tried, what failed, and why — while you still remember the why.

A note you can find in six months is worth more than an insight you can't reconstruct in six days.

What you'll find here

Three kinds of posts. Research notes: reading summaries, open questions, small results that don't justify a paper. Experiment logs: what I ran, with enough detail to rerun it. Tutorials: the things I had to figure out the hard way, written so you don't have to.

The ground rules

Everything here is a draft of my current understanding, not a claim of truth. When I change my mind, I update the post and note the change. If you spot an error, tell me — that's the point of writing in public.
